Use Strong, Unique Passwords
⌄

Most people use one simple password for everything. If that one password gets stolen, attackers can access all your accounts at once.

  • Use a passphrase — something like "Pizza!OnFridays2024" is long, easy to remember, and very hard to crack.
  • Never reuse the same password across different sites.
  • Use a password manager like Bitwarden (free) to store them securely.

Watch Out for Phishing
⌄

Phishing is when attackers send fake emails or messages pretending to be someone you trust — like your bank, Netflix, or even your school — to trick you into giving up your login info.

  • Check the sender's actual email address, not just the display name.
  • Hover over links before clicking to see where they really go.
  • If something feels urgent or too good to be true, it probably is.
"Phishing scams on social media platforms like Instagram are rising, with many users unaware of how to identify them." → See References — Phishing Scams on Social Media (2025)
Be Careful What You Share on Social Media
⌄

It is easy to accidentally share information that hackers can use against you. Your birthday, hometown, school, or pet's name might be the answer to your security questions.

  • Set your profiles to private.
  • Avoid posting things like your address, daily schedule, or travel plans.
  • Be selective about who you accept as a follower or friend.
"Users' awareness of phishing on social networks is still comparatively low compared with email-based attacks." → See References — Phishing Scams on Social Media (2025)
Keep Your Software Updated
⌄

It is annoying, but updates matter. Most software updates include security patches that fix vulnerabilities attackers actively look for in older versions.

  • Enable automatic updates on your phone and computer.
  • Keep your apps, browser, and operating system up to date.
  • Do not ignore update notifications — they are there for a reason.
Be Careful on Public Wi-Fi
⌄

Public Wi-Fi at coffee shops, airports, or libraries is convenient but risky. Anyone on the same network can potentially intercept what you are doing.

Turn On Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)
⌄

Two-factor authentication means that even if someone steals your password, they still cannot get into your account without a second step — usually a code sent to your phone.

  • Enable 2FA on your email, social media, and banking accounts first.
  • Use an authenticator app like Google Authenticator instead of SMS when possible.
  • It takes 10 seconds to set up and stops most account takeovers.
